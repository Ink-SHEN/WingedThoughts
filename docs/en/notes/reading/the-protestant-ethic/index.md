# The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism

> Max Weber. First published in two parts in 1904–1905 in the *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft und Sozialpolitik*; revised in 1920 as the opening essay of the *Collected Essays on the Sociology of Religion*.

## Bibliographic note

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Author | Max Weber (1864–1920), German sociologist and economist |

## In one sentence

Weber is **not** asking why people are greedy. He is asking a narrower and much harder question:

> Why was it precisely in the modern West that a way of life emerged which treats profit-making as an ethical duty and systematically restrains its own desires?

His answer: the ascetic ethic of post-Reformation Protestantism supplied the *psychological drive* behind the spirit of capitalism — and once the religious shell fell away, the mechanism it produced turned into a self-sustaining compulsion.

## How to read them

- **One pass:** read 1 → 2 → 3 → 4; together they cover the book's skeleton.
- **For method:** focus on the closing pages of volume one on the "ideal type" and "elective affinity" — that is the hinge of Weber's whole sociology.
- **For the controversy:** read the criticisms at the end of entry IV, then return to II and III — you can feel where Weber himself knew the evidence was thin.
